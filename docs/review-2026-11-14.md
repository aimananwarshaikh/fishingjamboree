# End-of-B1 review

Written 2026-11-14, in a fresh session, by a reviewer who had not seen the code. Nothing was fixed; this is a list for the designer and for the next build stages. (The previous review, `docs/review-2026-11-17.md`, is named after its handoff date and is the older of the two.)

Scope: everything merged after the `milestone-a` tag (PRs #17 to #23): the online play rules, the reconnect budget, room isolation, saved rooms, the Golden Hook Awards and the P3 fixes.

## Summary

- **No rule bug found** against `docs/rulebook.md` and the notes log. Three readings of the rules are worth the designer's eye (P2-3, and two small ones under P3).
- **No leak of hidden information found.** The seed, RNG state, deck order, the drawn awards and the stats do not reach a client before the result on any path I could find.
- **Replay and restore match** in every path I tried, including kicks, missed turns and a last-player ending.
- **The main risk is how fast the online rules punish a dropout.** A player who is gone for about a minute and a half is removed for good (P1-1), and once a player's 2-minute reconnect budget is spent, a one-second blip on their own turn loses the turn (P2-2).
- **The awards work as specified, but several rarely pay**, and the budget rule halves how often the two biggest awards are drawn (P2-4). These are design numbers for the designer, not code bugs.

Priorities: P1 = could spoil the first playtest. P2 = fix before playtests with people outside the team, or a design decision. P3 = cleanup, hardening, missing tests.

How each finding was checked: **read** (from the code) or **run** (measured with a throwaway script that is not in the repo, driving the real engine and the real `ServerCore` with the test harness's hand-wound clock). The award numbers come from 1,000 seeded games of 2 to 4 players, played twice: by a **random** bot (uniform over legal actions) and by a **cautious** bot (avoids risk tiles when it can, docks with 3 or more fish and on the last round, buys an upgrade half the time). Neither is a good player; the numbers show direction, not balance.

## P1

### 1. A dropout of about a minute and a half removes a player for good

- **Where:** `packages/server/src/core.ts:743` (an away player's turn is missed 2 seconds after the previous action), `packages/engine/src/apply.ts:222` (3 misses in a row remove the player), `core.ts:503-505` (a removed player's token answers `kicked`), `apps/web/src/connection.ts:164-167` (the client then forgets the seat).
- **What happens:** after the 60-second grace the player is away, and each of their turns is missed 2 seconds after it starts. "Three missed turns in a row" then takes only as long as the other players need for their own turns. Removal cannot be undone: the ship leaves the board, the player cannot rejoin and cannot win.
- **Checked by:** run. One player drops; the others take 5 seconds per action.

  | Players | Removed after | Then                                 |
  | ------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------ |
  | 2       | 84 s          | The game ends; the other player wins |
  | 3       | 104 s         | The game goes on without them        |
  | 4       | 124 s         | The game goes on without them        |

- **Why P1:** a laptop that sleeps, a phone call, or a Wi-Fi router restart is longer than this. In a first playtest of a 40-minute game, one such event takes a player out for good, and in a two-player game it ends the game. The designer decided "3 missed turns in a row"; the speed comes from combining that with the 2-second miss for away players, and I do not think it was seen as a number.
- **Options for the designer (not built):** count only turns that ran the full 90 seconds towards removal; or give an away player's turn a longer delay; or let a player removed for missed turns (not one the host kicked) come back; or leave removal to the host only.

## P2

### 2. With the reconnect budget spent, a short blip on your own turn loses the turn

- **Where:** `core.ts:882` (`vacate`: away at once when `graceLeftMs` is 0), `core.ts:743` (the away player's turn is missed 2 seconds after the _previous action_, which is usually already in the past).
- **What happens:** every disconnect uses up to 60 seconds of a 2-minute budget per game. When it is gone, a disconnect makes the player away at once. If it is their turn, the server misses it on the next tick, however short the drop was. The client's first reconnect attempt comes 0.5 seconds after the drop (`apps/web/src/connection.ts:229`), which is already too late.
- **Checked by:** run. A player used 118 seconds of budget in two drops, came back, and dropped a third time for 2 seconds (away). Later, on their own turn, after thinking for 5 seconds, the socket dropped: the next server step was already 3 seconds overdue and `missTurn` was applied at once.
- **Why it matters:** phones. A locked phone or a switch between Wi-Fi and mobile data kills the socket; two long locks spend the budget. From then on a network blip while deciding costs the turn, and on the last turn it also costs the awards (item 3).
- **Options:** always allow a short floor (say 10 seconds) before an away player's turn is missed, counted from the disconnect, not from the previous action; or refill some budget over time.

### 3. "Missed their last turn" takes the awards from a player who played it

- **Where:** `packages/engine/src/awards.ts:183-185` (`eligibleForAwards`: `missedTurns === 0`), `packages/engine/src/apply.ts:207-229` (`missTurn` counts in every phase, including the shop and the end-of-turn Helicopter choice).
- **What happens:** the designer's rule is "a player who is away when the game ends cannot win awards". The engine cannot see connections, so it reads this as "the player's last turn was missed" (notes log, 2026-11-14). Three cases follow that the designer may not expect:
  - **A full turn that ends on a prompt.** A player sails, docks and sells on their last turn. If they can afford an upgrade the shop stays open; with the Helicopter active the end-of-turn choice stays open. If they do not click **End turn** before the 90 seconds run out, the turn counts as missed and they are paid no awards, although everything they did stands.
  - **Away at the end, but paid.** A player who took their last turn and then left is paid.
  - **Present at the end, but not paid.** A player whose round-20 turn was missed and who reconnected before the last player finished is not paid.
- **Checked by:** run. In seed `c-20` (3 players) the last player is in the end-of-turn Helicopter choice: clicking **End turn** gives them $600 of awards, a total of $1,500 and the win; letting the timer run out gives $0 of awards, $900, and the win goes to another player. Seeds `c-10` and `c-39` show the same from the shop. With the cautious bot, 438 of 2,979 round-20 moves (14.7%) ended in the shop or the Helicopter choice.
- **Why P2, not P1:** the player has to sit for 90 seconds while everyone waits. It is still a wrong winner when it happens, and the timeout is likeliest on the very last action of the game, when players stop paying attention.
- **Options:** let a timeout in the shop or the end-of-turn choice end the turn without counting as missed; or have the server pass who is away into the end of the game so the rule can be applied as written.
- **Tests:** `awards.test.ts:347` covers the resolution with a hand-set `missedTurns`. No test goes through a real last turn that times out after the move.

### 4. Golden Hook Awards: numbers for the designer

All as specified in `docs/bonus-awards.md`; checked by run. Amounts are placeholders until B6, so the money figures matter less than the structural points.

- **Landlubber almost never pays** (`awards.ts:203-206`). It goes to the fewest Risk cards drawn, and 0 never wins, so it pays only when _every_ player has drawn at least one Risk card. When drawn, it paid nobody in 79% of random games and in 100% of cautious games (some player had 0 Risk draws in 754 and 1,000 of 1,000 games). The player it is meant to reward, the one who stays out of the danger areas, can never win it. `docs/next-steps.md` already flags this; the numbers are new.
- **"Fewest" awards and removed players.** A removed player still counts in the comparison (decision 6: no second place). A player removed early has the fewest tiles and usually 0 Risk draws, so Homebody goes unpaid and Landlubber pays nobody in any game with an early removal. Read, not run.
- **Golden Touch paid in 2% of the games it was drawn in** (both bots). In 34% of games no Golden Fish event is drawn at all (3 event cards from 11, 3 of them Golden Fish), so the award is dead before anyone moves. Real players will chase the fish more than these bots.
- **The budget rule skews the draw** (`awards.ts:128-144`, `content/awards.json:3`). The draw is uniform over the 364 valid sets of 3 (of 455). Daredevil is in 13.2% of them and Stripped Down in 11.3%; the other awards are in 18% to 24% (an even draw would be 20%). The two "major" awards that are meant to pull players into risk are drawn about half as often as the pity awards. With 2 players (101 valid pairs of 105) the skew is small.
- **Cautious players make Daredevil and Shark Bait pay nobody** (0% paid: nobody drew a Risk card). That is the rule working, but with fully secret awards nothing tells a first-time player that risk could pay.
- **Winner flips** (the winner on cash minus fine tokens differs from the final winner): 25.6% of random games, 13.6% of cautious games. By the gap between first and second before awards:

  | Gap before awards | Random bot | Cautious bot |
  | ----------------- | ---------- | ------------ |
  | $200 or less      | 223 of 475 | 115 of 286   |
  | $300 to $400      | 24 of 217  | 19 of 200    |
  | $500 to $900      | 9 of 237   | 2 of 323     |
  | $1,000 or more    | 0 of 71    | 0 of 191     |

  This matches the design goal (close games often decided, wide gaps never).

- **One player took every drawn award** in 15.2% (random) and 13.0% (cautious) of games.
- **Size:** the mean award money paid per game was $605 (random) and $535 (cautious); the median winning total was $500 and $1,600. Against the doc's rule of thumb of 10% to 15% of a winner's cash per award, the placeholders are large. For B6.

### 5. A room closed after a server error leaves nothing to debug with

- **Where:** `core.ts:922-931` (`closeBroken` calls `deleteRoom`), `core.ts:902-905` (which marks the room for removal from the store), `packages/server/src/transport.ts:184-190`.
- **What happens:** when a room throws in `tick` it is closed and its saved file is deleted. The only record is the stack trace in the log. The replay log (seed and actions), which would reproduce the bug in a test, is gone. Without `ROOMS_DIR` it never existed on disk.
- **Checked by:** read.
- **Why P2:** if this fires in a playtest, the game is lost either way; losing the replay as well makes the bug much harder to find. Keeping the file (for example as `<CODE>.json.bad`) or logging the replay log on close would cost little.

## P3

### Server

- **A removed seat is treated as disconnected after an empty room is rejoined** (`core.ts:861-873`, `occupy` does not skip removed seats). Run: after a kick, with both other players dropping and one rejoining, the removed seat showed `awayInMs: 60000`; 60 seconds later it became `away: true`, with a `room` message and `rev` going up. The same happens after every server restart to a room with a removed seat, since a restored room starts empty. The client shows "removed" first (`apps/web/src/App.tsx:439`), so players see nothing; it is wrong state and a spurious message.
- **A throw after an action was stored leaves the room unusable for up to 90 seconds** (`core.ts:644-662`: `commit` stores the state and the log entry, then builds the update; `core.ts:193-204` drops the update if that throws). Run, with `redactForClient` made to throw once: the sender got `server-error`, the server was at seq 1 and both clients at seq 0, every retry was answered `stale`, and nothing was sent until the turn timer fired 90 seconds later (an update at seq 2, which makes clients `sync`). The client treats `stale` as "the newer view is on its way" and does nothing (`apps/web/src/store.ts:236-242`). `roomIsolation.test.ts:157` only covers a throw before anything is stored. A client that sends `sync` after `stale` would recover at once.
- **The only connected player can restart their own turn timer** (`core.ts:758-763`): the timer counts from the later of the turn start and the moment an empty room was rejoined. Run: 80 seconds into a turn, with the other player disconnected, a drop and rejoin gave `turnTimeLeftMs: 90000`. Only matters when one player is alone in the room.
- **A player who rolls and then walks away is never removed automatically** (`apply.ts:255`: any own action resets `missedTurns`). They cost 90 seconds per turn until the host removes them. Read; as specified ("any action of the player's own resets the count").
- **A missed setup placement counts as one of the three missed turns** (`apply.ts:211`, tested in `missedTurns.test.ts:107`). The rulebook says "miss 3 turns in a row"; a placement is not a turn. A reading for the designer.
- **A player removed from the lobby can join again at once by code** (run: `kick`, then `join` with the same name succeeded). No ban list. Fine among friends; relevant once strangers can find rooms (B3, B5).
- **Names:** `cleanName` drops every format character (`core.ts:1043`), including the zero-width joiner, so emoji sequences such as a family or a flag-with-modifier split into separate emoji, and the zero-width non-joiner, which Persian spelling needs. Read.
- **Wording left from the old rule** ("the server plays for them"; it now only misses turns): `apps/web/src/describe.ts:184` (shown to players as the tooltip of "away"), `apps/web/src/store.ts:95`, `core.ts:110`, `docs/protocol.md:75`.

### Saved rooms

- **Messages are sent before the room is saved** (`transport.ts:162-163`), and the write is not flushed to disk (`packages/server/src/roomStore.ts:58-59`: write, rename, no `fsync`). A crash in between restores the game one action behind what clients saw. The client takes the `welcome` after the rejoin whole (`store.ts:183-199`), so play continues; a player may then choose again knowing the card that was drawn. The dice and cards repeat, so only a different move gains anything. A crash-only window; saving before sending would close it.
- **`.bad` files are never cleaned up** (`roomStore.ts:66-71`). They hold seat tokens and seeds, like the live files, and stay for ever.
- **A file's name is not checked against the code inside it** (`roomStore.ts:38-52`, `core.ts:340-348`). A copied file `ABCD.json` holding room `WXYZ` is restored as `WXYZ`, and the real `WXYZ.json`, read later, is then refused and moved aside. Only by hand.
- **The content hash covers display text** (`packages/engine/src/content.ts:525`: the whole of `awards.json`, `cards.json`, …). Fixing a typo in an award's name between deploys makes every saved game unrestorable; clients then get `room-not-found` and forget their saved seats (`connection.ts:168-179`). An engine change that alters any state does the same through the replay check (`core.ts:365-367`). Known in outline (`docs/next-steps.md`, B5); worth a line in the deploy checklist: no deploy while games are running.
- **Each restart gives an untouched room a fresh 30 minutes** (`core.ts:401`), so a server that restarts more often than that never deletes abandoned rooms.
- **A failed save is logged and play goes on** (`transport.ts:177-183`). After a restart the file may then be many actions and `rev`s behind; a client holding a higher `rev` keeps its old `RoomInfo` until the room catches up (`store.ts:136-139`). Read.
- **`parseRoomSnapshot` does not check that the host's seat is not removed** (`packages/server/src/snapshot.ts:99`). Cannot come from the server's own files.

### Web client

- **No backoff after a refused rejoin when the socket itself opens** (`connection.ts:104` resets `attempts` on every open; `connection.ts:187-192`, `:226-237`). A rejoin that keeps answering `server-error`, or a server that accepts sockets and closes them, is retried every 0.5 seconds for ever by every affected tab, and each try logs a stack trace on the server. `connection.test.ts:247` checks only the first retry. Read.
- **`leave` on an open socket waits for `left`** (`connection.ts:141-149`). If the socket drops before the reply, the tab stays on the table with no retry, and a refresh rejoins the seat the player meant to leave. Read.
- **`?server=`:** only the origin is checked, and the whole address is used (`apps/web/src/serverUrl.ts:67-73`). I could not turn that into sending a token elsewhere: userinfo (`wss://allowed@evil`) and backslash forms parse to the real host and are refused. An allowed origin with a `#fragment` makes `new WebSocket` throw in some browsers, which breaks that page load only.
- **The turn countdown is only refreshed by game messages** (`store.ts:192`, `:217`, `:231`), not by `room`. When an away player comes back mid-turn, the others keep showing no countdown until the next action. Cosmetic.
- **For B2:** the next player's 90 seconds start in the same update as the previous player's last action (`core.ts:659-661`). Card and award animations will eat into them unless the client or server allows for it.

### Docs

- **Rulebook, Big Haul:** "each fish token that went onto the ship" reads as if the Golden Fish counts; the engine excludes it (`awards.ts:63-69`, notes log 2026-11-14). One of the two should say so.
- **`docs/bonus-awards.md`** still describes `bonusAwarded` and `content/bonuses.json` in "How it works" and "Engine and server notes"; "Built (B1b)" at the end has the real names.

## What I looked for and did not find

- **Rule bugs.** I checked the rulebook's "Online play" and "Golden Hook Awards" sections and the notes rows of 2026-11-13 and 2026-11-14 line by line against `apply.ts`, `awards.ts`, `economy.ts` and `core.ts`: the 90 seconds per whole turn; what a missed turn does in each phase; placement on the first dock; removal and turn order; setup with a removed player; the last player winning; removed players never winning; 2 or 3 awards; conflicts; the budget; ties; 0 never winning; no second place; award money not paying off fine tokens. All as written, apart from the readings in P2-3 and P3.
- **Hidden information.** `redactForClient` builds the view field by field and has no `awards`, `rng` or draw piles (`packages/engine/src/view.ts:15-49`). No event before `awardGiven` carries a stat or an award. `welcome`, `snapshot` and `update` all go through `redactForClient`; `RoomInfo` has no token. A restored room sends nothing by itself, and a snapshot never goes to a client. Error replies are fixed strings; stack traces go only to the log. Clients cannot send `missTurn` or `removePlayer` (`packages/server/src/parse.ts:24-42`). The awards' own RNG stream cannot be read from the dice.
- **Determinism and replay.** No clock, `Math.random` or key-order dependence in the engine; the server's clock only drives timers and is not in the replay log or the snapshot. The state has no number-like object keys, so the JSON comparison in `restoreRoom` is stable. A 172-action four-player game replayed to the same state (snapshot 10.6 KB, replay 7.4 ms). Kicks, missed turns and the last-player ending are ordinary logged actions and replay (`missedTurns.test.ts:285`, `:318`).
- **Stuck rooms, double actions, tight loops.** Every step `tick` runs moves its own deadline or removes itself (away, missed turn, lobby seat, idle room), so the loop in `core.ts:258-261` ends; a room that throws is closed after one try. A timer miss and a late intent cannot both apply (`seq` check). A restored room with nobody connected only waits for its idle deletion. Every room status has a way out.
- **Persistence.** Writes are a temporary file and a rename; half files, bad JSON, other content and a log that does not replay are all refused, logged and moved aside without stopping the server (`persistence.test.ts:167`). Room codes cannot escape the folder (`roomStore.ts:33-36`). Files are created `0600`.
- **Client.** The retry, `leave`, `kicked`, `room-closed`, `replaced`, `bad-token` and `room-not-found` paths in `connection.ts` do what the notes say, apart from the two points above. Saved seats are only offered, never rejoined without a click; a kicked or closed seat is forgotten in both stores.

Not done: I did not run `scripts/playtest-three-tabs.mjs` or use a browser, and I did not test the transport with real sockets beyond reading its tests.
